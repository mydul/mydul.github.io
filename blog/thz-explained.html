Terahertz waves in plain language

Between microwaves and infrared sits terahertz radiation: roughly 0.1–10 THz, or wavelengths from 3 mm down to 30 µm. It was overlooked for a long time because sources and detectors were hard to build.

Why it helps sensing

Many molecules, water included, interact strongly with THz waves, and the photons are too weak to ionise tissue. That makes THz attractive for biosensing, for example reading out liquids in tiny channels.

What my thesis did

I fabricated a metamaterial, a patterned metal structure that resonates at a chosen frequency, combined it with a microfluidic layer, and measured how the resonance shifts as a liquid flows through. Want to see where 1 THz lands? Try the converter.

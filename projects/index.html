Material Analysis & Evaluation

A structured database that links material, heat treatment and sensor performance.

⌄

🧾About

Torque sensors that work on a magnetic principle depend on the steel shaft they measure. This project built a reusable knowledge base that connects a shaft’s material and heat treatment with the sensor performance it gives, so that materials can be chosen from data.

⚠️Problem

Material choice was mostly based on isolated past tests, supplier advice and experience. Metallurgy literature describes hardness and microstructure, but it does not link them to sensor-specific figures such as sensitivity, hysteresis, rotation-dependent signal variation and temperature drift. No standard internal database existed, and the same heat treatment can improve one value while worsening another.

🧭Approach

  • Planned the work as work packages: literature research, shaft procurement and preparation, batch heat treatments, standardised evaluation and database build-up.
  • Standardised the specimen geometry, clamping and measurement sequence so that results stay comparable across materials.
  • Coordinated several heat-treatment routes and supplier batches, and tracked schedule, budget and technical risks, such as limited transfer between material families.
  • Moved the shortlisted materials through proof of concept (PoC) and design verification (DVP), then released the validated treatment procedures.

💼Value for your company

Replaces scattered test experience with a data-based decision tool. Teams reach feasibility and material decisions faster, with fewer trial-and-error loops, and I can run the same kind of campaign with external suppliers.

✅Summary

  • A reusable material-performance database and evaluation workflow now exists.
  • A shortlist of validated materials was identified and has been used successfully in later projects.
  • Decisions about materials are based on data instead of trial and error, which saves development effort.
  • A follow-up phase extends the work to microstructure and to improving customers’ existing materials.

Advanced Surface Engineering for Non-Magnetic Measurement Bodies

Making accurate torque measurement possible on lightweight and non-magnetic shafts.

⌄

🧾About

The sensor principle works best when the shaft itself is magnetically suitable. This project tested whether a thin surface modification can give other measurement bodies, for example aluminium, the needed sensing behaviour. Two surface modification routes were compared: a chemical surface treatment layer and an amorphous metal foil layer.

⚠️Problem

Lightweight and non-magnetic measurement bodies do not give a usable signal on their own. Chemical surface treatment is a known industrial process, but its use as a sensing layer for high-accuracy measurement was not established. The influence of thickness, temperature, base material and surface roughness was not understood well enough for a product. Reproducibility was also a risk, because the surface modificationsupplier runs several process steps.

🧭Approach

  • Defined the project goal: bring the best surface modification route to a technology readiness level suitable for a product team, with performance close to the established reference.
  • Planned surface modification batches with defined thicknesses and process variants, using comparable measurement bodies so that surface modification effects could be separated from shaft shape and roughness.
  • Ran measurement campaigns before and after coating, including temperature, position dependence and durability (peel-off) tests.
  • Managed the external surface modificationsupplier, tracked process-variation risks, and led the transition from PoC to design verification and the release of surface modificationprofiles.

💼Value for your company

Takes a risky new technology from idea through proof of concept to patent and a release-ready process, while managing a supplier whose process steps are outside the company.

✅Summary

  • One of the surface modificationapproaches led to a patent.
  • Defined process windows for thickness, chemistry, surface quality and pre- and post-treatment.
  • Opens torque sensing for lightweight measurement bodies without a major shaft redesign.
  • A follow-up project applies the method to steels that do not perform well on their own.

Conceptual Design of an Integrated Sensor

A sensor concept in which almost the whole shaft surface contributes to the signal.

⌄

🧾About

Most sensors of this type read the shaft only at a few local spots. The integral concept collects the signal around the whole shaft, so that variation caused by shaft rotation becomes a small second-order effect instead of a main error.

⚠️Problem

Local sensing makes the result sensitive to shaft position, misalignment and local material variation. Good accuracy then needs compensation, careful shaft placement or tight shaft requirements. No concept existed that promised much lower uncompensated variation and still met the size, cost and manufacturability limits of the product family.

🧭Approach

  • Compared sensor concepts analytically and by simulation, judging sensitivity and rotation-dependent variation separately.
  • Planned several prototype generations: coil heads, adapted electronics, new mechanical parts and firmware changes.
  • Coordinated shaft procurement, hardware tests and measurement campaigns, and tracked the risk that simulation gains would not carry over to the real system because of tolerances and EMC.
  • Led the project through PoC, patent application, design verification and handover to product development.

💼Value for your company

Leads innovation projects where a simulated gain must be proven in real hardware, protects the idea through the patent process and hands a clean result to product development.

✅Summary

  • The concept was proven in hardware and was protected through a patent application.
  • Gives a basis for a clear step down in raw rotation-dependent signal variation.
  • Evidence is ready for design verification and the handover to the product team.

Development of a Cost-Optimised Sensor Variant

Cutting the series cost of an existing sensor while keeping its performance.

⌄

🧾About

The aim was a version of an existing sensor that is much cheaper to produce, with similar performance and size, which can also be shown to customers as a demonstrator.

⚠️Problem

Design decisions made in pre-development strongly influence the later series price, but the cost drivers were hard to read from supplier offers. The risk was that the cost reduction would cost too much performance or increase sensitivity to electromagnetic interference.

🧭Approach

  • Wrote the project profile and defined the goals, benefits, risks and work packages.
  • Asked each team (hardware, firmware, testing) what it needed, then estimated effort per phase, personnel, risks and cost into one plan.
  • Prepared design-choice meetings with clear decision points, for example classical or filter-based angle evaluation, and with or without a bus interface.
  • Asked the electronics manufacturer how price drivers work, such as component count, assembly type and soldering process.
  • Compared three candidate versions to decide which one to finalise.

💼Value for your company

Brings a design-to-cost mindset: finds the real cost drivers, turns open questions into decisions with owners, and aligns hardware, firmware and test teams on one plan.

✅Summary

  • A clear cost model and a risk list that guided the design decisions.
  • Open questions were turned into concrete decisions, owners and action points.
  • Modularity and fewer special processes were set as design principles.

Embedded Sensor Integration in Complex Rotating Components

An agile three-month plan to improve a compact sensor design with two prototype generations.

⌄

🧾About

A sensor that fits inside a rotating hollow shaft, such as in a wheel-hub motor or hollow robotic arm or rotating torque tube, could open a new integration option and a new business case. The first version fits the space but shows low sensitivity and unstable results.

⚠️Problem

The installation space is fixed, so performance has to improve by better coil layout, positioning and electronics, not by making the sensor larger. The first version had no reproducible baseline, and the main causes of the weak signal were not known.

🧭Approach

  • Structured the work in Azure DevOps as one epic, two features, seven user stories and six sprints, each with a goal, acceptance criteria and a definition of done.
  • Set three milestones: design and order, first prototype tested, both prototypes evaluated.
  • Let simulation, design, procurement and testing overlap, so that the second prototype does not wait for the first one’s full results.
  • Defined escalation rules: delays are accepted only after an escalation meeting about resource availability.
  • Planned a final comparison and a go / conditional go / no-go recommendation with a prioritised follow-up backlog.

💼Value for your company

Moves a team from a long waterfall plan to agile delivery, with clear milestones, acceptance criteria, escalation rules and a reviewable result in every sprint.

✅Summary

  • A traceable plan with reviewable results at the end of every sprint.
  • Moves a former waterfall-style plan to agile delivery.
  • The project is in progress. The result will be a recommendation for the next development phase.

M.Sc. thesis

THz metamaterial microfluidic biosensor chip

A label-free sensor chip for tiny liquid samples, fabricated and measured at THz frequencies.

⌄
Where
University of SiegenInstitute of High Frequency and Quantum Electronics
When
Completed May 2023
Tools & topics
PhotolithographyRIELift-offCVD / PVDPDMSTHz-TDS

🧾About

Terahertz (THz) waves are very sensitive to what a material is made of, which makes them attractive for biosensing without any labels or dyes. The thesis designed, built and measured a chip that combines a metamaterial sensor with a microfluidic channel for liquid samples.

⚠️Problem

Biosensing needs a sensitive, label-free readout. THz waves react strongly to liquids, but water absorbs them heavily, so the liquid volume must be tiny and well controlled. That asks for a sensor and a fluidic channel built together at very small scale.

🧭Approach

  • Fabricated metamaterial sensors in the cleanroom with photolithography, plasma/RIE etching, lift-off, and CVD/PVD deposition.
  • Built the microfluidic layer by soft imprinting with PDMS and TMCS surface treatment, so the liquid is confined to a thin, defined channel.
  • Checked the quality of each layer with optical microscopy and optical profilometry.
  • Measured the chips with THz frequency-domain spectroscopy for precise material characterisation. A HQ+ 2D graphene transfer system was also used in the lab work.

✅Summary

  • Working chips were fabricated and measured at THz frequencies.
  • The soft-imprint approach for the microfluidic layer was developed independently.
  • Combined cleanroom fabrication, polymer casting and THz measurement in one project.
  • The thesis was graded Very Good.

B.Sc. thesis

Memristor: window functions and logic gates

A comparison of memristor window functions and hybrid CMOS/memristor logic gates.

⌄
Where
Green University of BangladeshElectrical and Electronic Engineering
When
Completed May 2016
Tools & topics
Circuit simulationMRLCMOSMemristor models

🧾About

The memristor is a two-terminal device whose resistance depends on the charge that has flowed through it. It is a candidate building block for compact memory and logic. The thesis studied how it is modelled and how it can be used to build logic gates.

⚠️Problem

Memristor models need window functions to keep the internal state within its physical bounds, and different windows give different behaviour near the edges. Circuit designers also need to know whether memristor-based gates can compete with ordinary CMOS gates.

🧭Approach

  • Ran a comparative study of the common window functions that model the memristor’s boundary behaviour.
  • Designed logic gates in Memristor Ratioed Logic (MRL) style.
  • Built a hybrid CMOS/memristor cell that can be used for universal logic gates.
  • Compared the resulting gates with CMOS implementations in simulation.

✅Summary

  • Window functions were compared side by side as part of the thesis.
  • An XOR gate in the hybrid design was comparable to a CMOS implementation.
  • The work covered both the device model and circuit-level logic design.

Survey on Electrical Design Automation Tools and Software

A guided comparison of free/open-source and proprietary EDA tools.

⌄
Where
Green University of BangladeshDhaka, Bangladesh
When
February 2017
Published in
GUB Journal of Science and Engineering (GUBJSE), Vol. 1, Issue 1Green University Press · ISSN 2409-0476
Team
Md. Hasan Maruf lead authorMd. Mydul Islam co-authorFaysal Al Mahmud co-author
Tools & topics
EDAVLSIFPGAOpen source

🧾About

The paper surveys the software tools used in electronic design automation (EDA), from system level down to layout, and compares free/open-source tools with proprietary suites. It is a literature and tool survey, with no lab experiments.

⚠️Problem

There are many EDA tools and they differ a lot. Free tools rarely cover every design step, so users combine several of them. Proprietary suites cover more but come with licences and cost. Students and engineers need a clear picture before they choose.

🧭Approach

  • Grouped the tools by function: schematic entry, PCB design, simulation, synthesis, physical layout and FPGA work.
  • Split them into open-source (e.g. KiCad, Magic, Icarus Verilog, QUCS) and proprietary (Synopsys, Cadence, Mentor Graphics, Tina).
  • Compared the two groups on development, support, flexibility, cost, features, accuracy and efficiency.

✅Summary

  • Proprietary suites lead on features, accuracy, efficiency and support.
  • Free tools cost nothing and can be modified, but a full flow often needs several of them.
  • The best choice depends on the user’s needs, budget and the support the project requires.

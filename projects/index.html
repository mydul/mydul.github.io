M.Sc. thesis

THz metamaterial microfluidic biosensor chip

A label-free sensor chip for tiny liquid samples, fabricated and measured at THz frequencies.

⌄
Where
University of SiegenInstitute of High Frequency and Quantum Electronics
When
Completed May 2023
Tools & topics
PhotolithographyRIELift-offCVD / PVDPDMSTHz-TDS

🧾About

Terahertz (THz) waves are very sensitive to what a material is made of, which makes them attractive for biosensing without any labels or dyes. The thesis designed, built and measured a chip that combines a metamaterial sensor with a microfluidic channel for liquid samples.

⚠️Problem

Biosensing needs a sensitive, label-free readout. THz waves react strongly to liquids, but water absorbs them heavily, so the liquid volume must be tiny and well controlled. That asks for a sensor and a fluidic channel built together at very small scale.

🧭Approach

  • Fabricated metamaterial sensors in the cleanroom with photolithography, plasma/RIE etching, lift-off, and CVD/PVD deposition.
  • Built the microfluidic layer by soft imprinting with PDMS and TMCS surface treatment, so the liquid is confined to a thin, defined channel.
  • Checked the quality of each layer with optical microscopy and optical profilometry.
  • Measured the chips with THz frequency-domain spectroscopy for precise material characterisation. A HQ+ 2D graphene transfer system was also used in the lab work.

✅Summary

  • Working chips were fabricated and measured at THz frequencies.
  • The soft-imprint approach for the microfluidic layer was developed independently.
  • Combined cleanroom fabrication, polymer casting and THz measurement in one project.
  • The thesis was graded Very Good.

B.Sc. thesis

Memristor: window functions and logic gates

A comparison of memristor window functions and hybrid CMOS/memristor logic gates.

⌄
Where
Green University of BangladeshElectrical and Electronic Engineering
When
Completed May 2016
Tools & topics
Circuit simulationMRLCMOSMemristor models

🧾About

The memristor is a two-terminal device whose resistance depends on the charge that has flowed through it. It is a candidate building block for compact memory and logic. The thesis studied how it is modelled and how it can be used to build logic gates.

⚠️Problem

Memristor models need window functions to keep the internal state within its physical bounds, and different windows give different behaviour near the edges. Circuit designers also need to know whether memristor-based gates can compete with ordinary CMOS gates.

🧭Approach

  • Ran a comparative study of the common window functions that model the memristor’s boundary behaviour.
  • Designed logic gates in Memristor Ratioed Logic (MRL) style.
  • Built a hybrid CMOS/memristor cell that can be used for universal logic gates.
  • Compared the resulting gates with CMOS implementations in simulation.

✅Summary

  • Window functions were compared side by side as part of the thesis.
  • An XOR gate in the hybrid design was comparable to a CMOS implementation.
  • The work covered both the device model and circuit-level logic design.

Journal paper · Co-author

Survey on Electrical Design Automation Tools and Software

A guided comparison of free/open-source and proprietary EDA tools.

⌄
Where
Green University of BangladeshDhaka, Bangladesh
When
February 2017
Published in
GUB Journal of Science and Engineering (GUBJSE), Vol. 1, Issue 1Green University Press · ISSN 2409-0476
Team
Md. Hasan Maruf lead authorMd. Mydul Islam co-authorFaysal Al Mahmud co-author
Tools & topics
EDAVLSIFPGAOpen source

🧾About

The paper surveys the software tools used in electronic design automation (EDA), from system level down to layout, and compares free/open-source tools with proprietary suites. It is a literature and tool survey, with no lab experiments.

⚠️Problem

There are many EDA tools and they differ a lot. Free tools rarely cover every design step, so users combine several of them. Proprietary suites cover more but come with licences and cost. Students and engineers need a clear picture before they choose.

🧭Approach

  • Grouped the tools by function: schematic entry, PCB design, simulation, synthesis, physical layout and FPGA work.
  • Split them into open-source (e.g. KiCad, Magic, Icarus Verilog, QUCS) and proprietary (Synopsys, Cadence, Mentor Graphics, Tina).
  • Compared the two groups on development, support, flexibility, cost, features, accuracy and efficiency.

✅Summary

  • Proprietary suites lead on features, accuracy, efficiency and support.
  • Free tools cost nothing and can be modified, but a full flow often needs several of them.
  • The best choice depends on the user’s needs, budget and the support the project requires.

Lab project · M.Sc.

Cross-sectioning PEM fuel cells (ultramicrotome)

Preparing ultra-thin slices of fuel cell electrodes for TEM analysis.

⌄
Where
University of SiegenResearch-group cleanroom and labs
When
During M.Sc. studies, up to May 2023
Tools & topics
UltramicrotomyTEM sample prepOptical microscopy

🧾About

A transmission electron microscope (TEM) can only image extremely thin slices. This project prepared such slices from PEM fuel cell electrodes with an ultramicrotome, a machine that cuts sections with a diamond or glass knife.

⚠️Problem

The layers of a fuel cell are soft, fragile and different from each other, so cutting a clean cross-section is hard. The knife angle and the knife quality strongly affect the result.

🧭Approach

  • Researched the effect of diamond and glass knife angles on the cut.
  • Examined knives and sections with optical microscopy.
  • Designed, prototyped and implemented a glass knife boat, the small water trough that holds the sections after cutting.
  • Prepared cross-sections of PEM fuel cell electrodes for TEM analysis.

✅Summary

  • Covered the whole sample-preparation chain for TEM, from knife to section.
  • Included a hands-on design task: the glass knife boat.
  • Showed how much preparation quality decides the quality of nanoscale imaging.

From CVD-grown graphene to a working transistor and its electrical measurement.

Where

When
During M.Sc. studies, up to May 2023
Tools & topics
CVDOptical lithographyPVD evaporationMobility measurement

🧾About

Graphene is a one-atom-thick carbon layer with very high carrier mobility, which makes it interesting for fast transistors and sensors. This project built a graphene transistor and measured how well it works.

⚠️Problem

Graphene transistor performance depends heavily on fabrication quality, so each step has to be controlled and then verified by measurement.

🧭Approach

  • Grew graphene by chemical vapour deposition (CVD).
  • Patterned the devices with optical lithography.
  • Deposited thin-film contacts by evaporation (PVD).
  • Measured the carrier mobility and transconductance of the finished transistor.

✅Summary

  • Covered the full chain: material growth, device fabrication and electrical characterisation.
  • Produced working GFET devices that were measured for mobility and transconductance.
  • Linked cleanroom process quality directly to device performance.

Lab project · M.Sc.

Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) sensing

Studying a label-free optical sensing method at the water–gold interface.

⌄
Where
University of SiegenPhotonics lab
When
During M.Sc. studies, up to May 2023
Tools & topics
SPROttoKretschmannOptics

🧾About

Surface plasmon resonance detects tiny changes at a metal surface, for example when molecules attach to it. It is widely used as a label-free biosensing method.

⚠️Problem

To use SPR for sensing, one has to understand how light couples into the plasmon at the metal–medium interface, and how that depends on polarisation and set-up.

🧭Approach

  • Carried out a graphical analysis of the water–gold interface.
  • Measured P- and S-polarised light with an air–gold medium.
  • Compared the Otto and Kretschmann configurations for exciting the plasmon.

✅Summary

  • Showed the polarisation dependence of plasmon excitation (P versus S).
  • Compared the two classic coupling set-ups.
  • Gave the optical background for later THz and biosensor work.

X-ray fluorescence (XRF) analysis

Identifying the elements in a sample with energy- and wavelength-dispersive XRF.

⌄
Where
University of SiegenMaterials characterisation lab
When
During M.Sc. studies, up to May 2023
Tools & topics
EDXRFWDXRFSDDCalibration

🧾About

XRF tells which chemical elements are in a sample without destroying it. The sample is excited with X-rays and emits fluorescence at energies that are specific to each element.

⚠️Problem

Reading an XRF spectrum correctly needs a calibrated detector, and the two detection principles, energy-dispersive and wavelength-dispersive, behave differently.

🧭Approach

  • Worked with both energy-dispersive (ED) and wavelength-dispersive (WD) XRF.
  • Calibrated the energy axis of the silicon drift detector (SDD).
  • Carried out qualitative multi-element analysis of samples.

✅Summary

  • Learned the full XRF routine: calibration first, then analysis.
  • Compared the two dispersive methods on real samples.
